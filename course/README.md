# PHI 215 Symbolic Logic — Fall 2026

**Format:** Asynchronous and remote  
**Credits:** 3  
**Semester:** Fall 2026  
**Instructor:** John Beverley

## Course Description

This course develops skills of rigorous argumentation and careful reasoning through the basic techniques of symbolic deductive logic.

Students will learn to symbolize ordinary English sentences in a constructed logical notation, make steps in reasoning explicit, and test arguments using truth tables, natural deduction systems, and semantic interpretations. The course also addresses formal and informal fallacies, reductio ad absurdum, quantificational logic, identity, and selected extensions of predicate logic.

The course is cumulative. Later topics build directly on earlier techniques.

## Course Format

The course is **asynchronous**. There are no scheduled weekly class meetings.

Students should work through the material using the 15-week sequence in the [Fall 2026 Schedule](schedule.md). Each week identifies the relevant topics, readings, and the recommended assessment milestone.

All graded homework assignments and exams are submitted through Microsoft Forms.

The Forms are **open throughout the semester**. Students may work ahead and may submit an assignment whenever they are prepared to do so. The weekly assessment entries are recommendations for keeping pace with the course rather than restrictions on when a Form can be accessed.

## Main Areas of Study

### Arguments and Basic Logical Concepts

Students will learn to:

- distinguish arguments from non-arguments;
- identify premises and conclusions;
- explain validity, soundness, truth, contradiction, and logical form;
- extract arguments from ordinary language; and
- distinguish deductive from inductive reasoning.

### Propositional Logic

Students will learn to:

- translate between ordinary language and propositional logic;
- construct and evaluate truth tables;
- test arguments for validity;
- use rules of inference;
- construct direct, conditional, and indirect derivations; and
- recognize tautologies, contradictions, and contingencies.

### Predicate Logic

Students will learn to:

- use quantifiers and predicates;
- translate quantified statements;
- work with the scope of quantifiers and connectives;
- construct and evaluate predicate-logic derivations;
- use universal and existential inference rules; and
- evaluate semantic interpretations.

### Extensions of Predicate Logic

Students will apply predicate logic to:

- identity;
- parthood; and
- selected formal properties of relations.
