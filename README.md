## Logic Competency Exam

This page hosts materials used in the University at Buffalo, Department of Philosophy Logic Competency Exam. Exerpt from the [Philosophy Graduate Handbook](https://www.buffalo.edu/cas/philosophy/grad-study/graduate-handbook.html) and the [Applied Ontology Graduate Handbook](http://ontology.buffalo.edu/docs/UB-AO-Masters-Handbook.pdf)

### Student Requirements

Every student must either (1) pass the Symbolic Logic Competency Exam before graduating or (2) receive a B+ or better in an Independent Study covering Symbolic Logic. Previous logic courses cannot satisfy the Symbolic Logic Requirement.  

The Symbolic Logic Competency Exam is offered twice per year: the week before Fall semester classes begin and the week before Spring semester classes begin. 

Every incoming first-year student must take the Symbolic Logic Competency Exam prior to starting classes. Incoming students who do not pass the Symbolic Logic Competency Exam will be required to take it the next time it is offered. 

Students who fail to pass the Symbolic Logic Competency Exam after two attempts must pass an Independent Study in Symbolic Logic with a grade of B+ or higher prior to graduation. 

### Exam Logistics

The exam will contain material equivalent to that found in the UB PHI 215 undergraduate symbolic logic course, i.e. up to first-order predicate logic with identity, symbolizations, derivations, proofs of invalidity, semantic interpretations, etc. In the Exams directory you will find a practice exam and solutions. Above you will find links two textbooks which were used to develop the exam. The relevant sections from Hardegree cover symbolizations and proof theory; the relevant sections of Bennett cover semantic interpretations. 
 
You will have one hour to complete the exam; it will be taken in person. In extraordinary circumstances, you may be permitted to take the exam at the regularly scheduled time over zoom.

## Suggested Weekly Schedule

* **Week 1** Course introduction, arguments, form vs. content
  - Hardegree 1.1–1.2, 1.4–1.5

* **Week 2** Induction vs. deduction, fallacies, definitions
  - Hardegree 1.5, 2.1

* **Week 3** Truth tables I, logical connectives
  - Hardegree 2.1–2.10

* **Week 4** Truth tables II, tautologies, contradictions
  - Hardegree 2.5–2.13

* **Week 5** Sentential logic, testing for validity
  - Hardegree 3.1–3.4

* **Week 6** Proof theory for sentential logic I
  - Hardegree 3.1–3.4

* **Week 7** Proof theory for sentential logic II
  - Hardegree 5.1–5.5

* **Week 8** Conditional derivations, complex inference
  - Hardegree 5.7–5.9

* **Week 9** Indirect derivations I
  - Hardegree 5.10–5.11

* **Week 10** Indirect derivations II
  - Hardegree 5.12–5.14

* **Week 11** Quantifiers and quantificational logic
  - Hardegree 6.1–6.4
  - Bennett 6.1-6.3

* **Week 12** Translating quantifiers, scope and limitations
  - Hardegree 6.5–6.17
  - Bennett 7.1-7.3

* **Week 13** Quantifier inference rules
  - Hardegree 8.1–8.12

* **Week 14** Identity and extensions of predicate logic
  - Bennett 8.1-8.4
